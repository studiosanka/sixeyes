# Universal Joint PCB — Design Reference
**Project:** 6-DOF Robotic Arm — Modular Backplane Controller
**Board:** 4-layer, one design deployed to all joint nodes, **fully through-hole / socketed modules, hand-solderable** (size TBD from the Super Mini and TMC2209 footprints — no longer 42×42 mm)
**Revision:** v1.2 — a new document; the superseded v1.1 reference is kept in the repository history (branch `archive/dev-2026-10`)

> **Design goal:** a builder buys the bare PCB and solders every part by hand. No SMD, no reflow, no stencil. Total arm budget is SGD 250 (prints, mechanics, electronics; PSU and compute excluded).
> **BOM:** [v1.2_BOM_LCSC.csv](../../hardware/pcb/bom/v1.2_BOM_LCSC.csv) is the same list as §10 in a format LCSC's BOM tool can import.

## 1. Architecture

| Node | Populated | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Base | Stepper 1 driver, USB to host, CAN master, CAN termination jumper fitted | Bus master, host bridge |
| Shoulder L | Stepper 2A driver | CAN node |
| Shoulder R | Stepper 2B driver | CAN node, inverse-direction logic |
| Elbow | Stepper 3 driver, 3× servo headers (open-loop), CAN termination jumper fitted | CAN node, closest hop to wrist/gripper |
| Spare | — | Unpopulated spare board |

Every board is identical; the variant is set by what you solder (servo headers, termination shunt) and by `-DJOINT_NODE_ID` in firmware. Servos are **open-loop**: PWM out only.

Motors: 4× NEMA17 (40 mm class), all behind 1:25 printed cycloidal reductions. See [GEARBOX_TORQUE_BUDGET.md](GEARBOX_TORQUE_BUDGET.md).

## 2. Power Architecture

```
24V IN (5.08mm screw terminal)
 ├─ P6KE30A TVS across 24V/GND
 ├─ TMC2209 module VM (direct) + 100uF/50V bulk
 └─ XL4016E1 buck + 22uH + MBR1060 catch diode ──► V6 = 6.42 V
        ├─ servo headers (Elbow)
        └─ LM2940CT-5.0 ──► V5 = 5.0 V
              ├─ MCP2562 VDD
              └─ 1N5819 ──► Super Mini "5V" pad
Super Mini "3V3" pad ──► V3V3: AS5600 module, TMC2209 VIO, MCP2562 VIO, EN pull-up
```

**Buck (XL4016E1, LCSC C55881).** Pins (datasheet): 1 GND, 2 FB, 3 SW, 4 VC, 5 VIN. Operating 8–36 V in, 40 V absolute max, 180 kHz, no EN pin, compensation internal.
- Output: `Vout = 1.25 × (1 + R2 / R1)` = 1.25 × (1 + 6.2k / 1.5k) = **6.42 V**. R2 is from V6 to FB, R1 from FB to GND, CFF (33 nF) across R2.
- CC (1 µF) sits between pin 5 (VIN) and pin 4 (VC), as in the datasheet reference circuit.
- Datasheet reference parts: 47 µH / 12 A inductor, MBR2045 diode, 470 µF/50 V in, 1000 µF/25 V out, plus 1 µF ceramics (C1, C2) at VIN and VOUT. v1.2 uses a Bourns SRP1265A-220M (22 µH, Isat 12 A, Irms 9 A, 32.5 mΩ; **SMD, hand-soldered**), MBR1060, **one** 470 µF/50 V in (CIN, 2.05 A ripple at 100 kHz, 27 mΩ), **one** 1000 µF/16 V out (COUT, 1.76 A, 28 mΩ), and 1 µF X7R ceramics C1 and C2. At 24→6.4 V and 180 kHz, 22 µH gives roughly 1.2 A peak-to-peak inductor ripple (about 6.6 A peak at 6 A load) and about 0.35 A RMS output-capacitor ripple (calculated, not bench-measured). That is still below the datasheet's 47 µH, so bench-check the 6.4 V rail for stability and ripple. Alternatives in the same Bourns family: -150M (C189912, 15 µH, more ripple) and -330M (C7369670, 8 A Irms, hotter, out of stock when checked).
- **CIN ripple:** input ripple current is about Iout × √(D(1−D)) ≈ 2.65 A RMS at 6 A and 3.1 A at 7 A, so the single CIN (2.05 A rating) is running near its limit during sustained servo load. Servo peaks are short so the average is lower; watch the can temperature on the bench. A 16 × 25 mm can would give more margin but I found no suitable LCSC part.
- **C1 and C2** (1 µF X7R) are the high-frequency decoupling: C1 across VIN pin 5 to GND pin 1 and C2 across V6 to GND, placed right at the IC pins and the inductor/diode loop. A 10 µF at VIN was considered and rejected: the only THT 10 µF/50 V part is an electrolytic, which adds no high-frequency benefit over C1.
- **The metal tab is the SW node.** A heatsink bolted to it must be electrically insulated (silicone pad + shoulder washer) and must not touch ground or anything else.
- Current: the datasheet advertises up to 12 A. Treat **5–6 A** as the usable continuous limit, same as the v1.1 derating, and fit a small heatsink if servos run hard. At 6 A out, expect on the order of 5 W lost across the IC, diode and inductor (estimate).

**5 V rail (LM2940CT-5.0/NOPB, C9413).** Dropout is typically 0.5 V at 1 A (datasheet), so a 6.42 V input has about 0.9 V of headroom. The output capacitor must be ≥ 22 µF with ESR between 0.1 Ω and 1 Ω: use the specified aluminium electrolytic (COUT_LD), not ceramic and not a polymer cap.

**Why 5 V is needed.** MCP2562 VDD is 4.5–5.5 V. Only the CAN transceiver and the Super Mini 5 V pad use it (a few hundred mA at most).

**Super Mini power and USB.** On common Super Mini boards the "5V" pad is tied straight to USB VBUS with no protection diode (one source says so; check your module). So V5 reaches the pad through **DLD1 (1N5819)**:
- Board powered, USB plugged in: the host's 5 V is higher than V5 minus the diode drop, DLD1 is reverse-biased, nothing flows into the host.
- USB only (bench flashing, no 24 V): the module runs from VBUS; V5 is off, MCP2562 VDD is unpowered (an unpowered node does not load the bus, per its datasheet).
- Do not wire the 3V3 pad of the Super Mini to a second regulator.

**TVS.** P6KE30A: 25.6 V standoff, 41.4 V clamp at rated surge. Caveats: TMC2209 absolute max VM is about 29 V and XL4016 absolute max Vin is 40 V, so a TVS this size limits big hot-plug transients but cannot fully protect either part. Use a regulated 24 V supply (not above ~26 V), fuse the supply output (suggest 8 A), and add reverse-polarity protection at the supply end. A TVS alone is **not** reverse-polarity protection.

**Current budget (estimates).** Four steppers at roughly 1–1.5 A on the 24 V side each, plus the servo rail at up to 6 A × 6.4 V ÷ 24 V ÷ 0.85 ≈ 1.9 A: about 6–8 A worst-case at the Base input, 3–4 A typical. See §9 for the connectors that carry this.

**Double-check summary (v1.1 → v1.2).**
- GPIOs 21, 22, 23 from v1.1 are **not used**: they are underside pads on the C6 Super Mini, not header pins. The same goes for GPIO12/13 (USB).
- SPI encoder pins 18–21 became I²C 18/19.
- CAN moved off 4/5 (strapping) to 14/20 (both safe).
- Servos took the "care" pins 6/7/4 instead.
- Added the signals v1.1 omitted: TMC2209 EN (3) and UART RX (17).
- v1.1's list of reserved pins (24–30) no longer applies; flash is in-package on the C6FH4 (Espressif's schematic checklist says in-package flash pins are not bonded out).
- The TMC2209 needs a separate RX pin: with TX and RX on the same GPIO the idle-high TX driver would hold the line and the reply would not be readable.
- GPIO16/17 are UART0's default pads. Serial is moved to the native USB CDC (`ARDUINO_USB_CDC_ON_BOOT=1` in `platformio.ini`) so UART0 never claims GPIO17.

**Not verified, confirm before layout:**
- Super Mini **dimensions** differ by seller (the C6's overall length is reported as 22.5, 25 or 27 mm). Pin positions are what matter for the socket; the overall length only affects clearance. Buy one sample from the exact listing, measure the row spacing and length, and confirm the pin order matches the table above. Keep the antenna end clear of copper and tall parts.
- That the 5 V pad is tied straight to USB VBUS on your module (continuity test on arrival).

## 4. USB

Each Super Mini has its own USB-C, so every node can be flashed and debugged directly, but only the Base node's USB is the host link in operation. There is no board-level USB circuit: no receptacle, no CC resistors, no ESD part (ESD protection dropped by design). Never connect USB with 24 V present unless §2's diode is fitted. Firmware maps `Serial` to this native USB CDC port (`ARDUINO_USB_CDC_ON_BOOT=1`).

## 5. CAN Bus

**MCP2562-E/P (LCSC C640345), PDIP-8, 1 Mbps.** Pinout from the datasheet: 1 TXD, 2 VSS, 3 VDD, 4 RXD, 5 VIO, 6 CANL, 7 CANH, 8 STBY.

| Pin | Connect to |
|---|---|
| TXD (1) | GPIO14 |
| VSS (2) | GND |
| VDD (3) | V5, with CCN1 100 nF |
| RXD (4) | GPIO20 |
| VIO (5) | V3V3, with CCN2 100 nF (VIO range 1.8–5.5 V) |
| CANL (6) / CANH (7) | CAN_L / CAN_H |
| STBY (8) | **GND (normal mode)** — STBY has an internal pull-up, so a floating pin leaves the part in standby |

- Linear daisy chain Base → Shoulder L → Shoulder R → Elbow. Termination (RCN1, 120 Ω) is switched in with the JCN3 jumper: fit the shunt on **Base and Elbow only**.
- Optional: a DIP-8 socket (not on the LCSC list).
- v1.1's RS slope resistor does not exist on this part.

## 6. Encoder (AS5600 module, I²C)

- **Module (as drawn in your photo):** two separate headers on the module. The module is **soldered straight to the board** (no sockets): its male header pins go through two slotted hole rows on the PCB. JEC1 and JEC2 below name those two pin rows; both are in one footprint.

| Socket | Module pin (top → bottom) | Net | Connect to |
|---|---|---|---|
| JEC1 (1×3) | 1 VCC | V3V3 | Super Mini 3V3 output (JMC2 pin 3) |
| | 2 OUT | — | Leave unconnected (analog/PWM output, unused) |
| | 3 GND | GND | Ground |
| JEC2 (1×4) | 1 DIR | GND | Tie to GND on the board (angle increases clockwise) |
| | 2 SCL | SCL | GPIO19 (JMC2 pin 5) |
| | 3 SDA | SDA | GPIO18 (JMC2 pin 6) |
| | 4 GPO (PGO) | — | Leave unconnected (OTP programming pin) |

- **Footprint (`SixEyes_custom:AS5600_Module_C_Solder_1x3_1x4`):** one footprint holds both pin rows, taken from a 3D model of the module that has its male headers modelled (GrabCAD). Three candidate models were compared; two agree on a row spacing of about 13.5 mm (13.47 and 13.5) and the third gives 13.0 mm, so 13.5 mm is used. Pins 1–3 (VCC, OUT, GND) are the 1×3 row on the left; pins 4–7 (DIR, SCL, SDA, GPO) are the 1×4 row on the right, and the 4-pin row sits about 0.4 mm toward the pin-1 end relative to the 3-pin row (all three models agree on this offset, but not on the board's size: 23.0 to 23.2 mm). Holes are slots, 1.6 × 1.0 mm long along X in 2.4 × 1.7 mm pads, so each pin can sit ±0.3 mm from nominal and the row spacing can be off by ±0.6 mm (a 13.0 mm module still seats). Because both rows must enter their holes at once, the slots matter more now that there are no sockets: solder one row, check the module is square and flat, then solder the other.
- **Not verified on a real module:** which end is pin 1 (assumed to be the same end for both rows), the real row spacing, and the board size. Measure with calipers before ordering boards. The module's header plastic (2.29 mm) rests on the PCB, so its underside is 2.3 mm above the board and the chip top is about 5.4 mm above it. The module's pins protrude about 4.2 mm below the PCB, so keep the underside clear under it. The 4 × 4 × 2 mm block above the chip in this STEP is a magnet drawn by the model's author, not part of the module.
- **Power and pull-ups (confirmed from the module's schematic):** R1 (0 Ω) ties the chip's VDD5V and VDD3V3 pins together, which is the AS5600's 3.3 V mode, so feed VCC with 3.3 V (never 5 V on this variant). R2 and R3 (10 kΩ) pull SCL and SDA up to VCC, so **no pull-ups are needed on the main board**. R4 (1 kΩ) holds GPO low. The module's pin order matches the tables above (P2: VCC, OUT, GND; P1: DIR, SCL, SDA, GPO). If your seller's module differs from this schematic (for example R1 not fitted), re-check before powering it.
- **I²C speed:** with 10 kΩ pull-ups the rise time is about 0.85 × 10 kΩ × bus capacitance, which is fine at 100 kHz and borderline at 400 kHz once the bus capacitance reaches roughly 100 pF (estimate, not measured). Firmware currently runs 400 kHz; if reads fail, drop `I2C_CLOCK_HZ` in `encoder_driver.cpp` to 100 kHz.
- Fixed I²C address 0x36, one per board, so no address conflicts.
- 12-bit (4096 counts per turn). On the motor shaft that is about 0.09° per count, enough for the encoder-vs-commanded stall check. A read is about 150–250 µs at 400 kHz (estimate), well inside a 4 ms tick at 250 Hz.
- Needs a diametrically magnetised magnet (typically 6 × 2.5 mm) centred on the shaft, 0.5–3 mm from the chip. That fixes where the board or module sits: either a dual-shaft motor with the board at the back, or the module on a short cable. NEMA17 stray fields can disturb the reading, so offset or shield.
- Pin order and headers vary by seller. The tables above match the module in the photo (1×3: VCC, OUT, GND on one side; 1×4: DIR, SCL, SDA, GPO on the other). If you buy a different module, redo the footprint to match its silkscreen.
- **Motor mounting (measured 2026-10-16):** the motor hangs on 4 × M3x8 female-female brass standoffs (LCSC C551328) that stand on top of the board. A screw from the PCB top passes through the board and the standoff and into the motor's tapped hole; the female thread in the motor is why a female-female standoff plus screw is used and not a male-female one.
  - The standoff end enters the 1.5 mm indentation around each tapped hole, so the motor's flat rear face is at **8 + 1.5 = 9.5 mm** above the board. Thread depth is about 4 mm from the recess floor.
  - Screw reach from the head to the recess floor is 1.6 (PCB) + 8 (standoff) = 9.6 mm. M3x12 gives only 2.4 mm of engagement (marginal); M3x14 gives 4.4 mm and bottoms out; **M3x14 with a 0.5 mm washer under the head gives 3.9 mm**, just under the thread depth.
  - Magnet gap: with the module on its header plastic, the chip top is 5.4 mm above the board. For the measured 2.5 mm shaft-to-IC gap the shaft/magnet end must be at 7.9 mm, i.e. it must protrude about **1.6 mm below the motor's rear face**; the AS5600 wants roughly 0.5–3 mm. Check this against the real rear shaft before ordering.
  - The motor STEP has 2.0 mm corner counterbores at the rear (the real motor has a 1.5 mm indentation with tapped holes), so the 3D view shows about 0.5 mm of overlap with the standoff tops. This is cosmetic.

## 7. Stepper Driver (TMC2209 module)

- Two 1×8 female headers (JDR1, JDR2), 12.7 mm (0.5") apart: the standard 2×8 StepStick footprint. **Module pin order is not identical across sellers** (the MS3/RESET/SLEEP positions carry PDN_UART/CLK/DIAG/INDEX differently). Check the silkscreen of the module you buy; I could not verify one reference module's pinout here.
- Nets used: EN (GPIO3, RDR2 pull-up to V3V3), MS1 and MS2 to GND (UART address 0), PDN_UART (GPIO2 via RDR1 1 kΩ; RX on GPIO17), STEP (GPIO0), DIR (GPIO1), VM (24 V), VIO (V3V3), GND ×2, motor 2B/2A/1A/1B.
**Per-pin wiring.** Pin number = position along each 1×8 header, starting at the end where EN and VM sit. JDR1 is the signal row, JDR2 the power/motor row.

> **Unverified order.** This follows the common StepStick-style TMC2209 layout (the older BIGTREETECH listing: EN, MS1, MS2, PDN_UART, PDN_UART, CLK, STEP, DIR over VM, GND, 2B, 2A, 1A, 1B, VIO, GND). I could not confirm it for the V1.3 or for your seller's module, where pins 4–6 and the UART pad vary. Check the module's silkscreen before drawing the symbol. JDR3's coil order (pins 1–4 = 1A, 1B, 2A, 2B) is also an assumption: confirm coil pairs with a meter.

**JDR1 (signal row)**

| Pin | Module pad | Net | Connect to |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | EN | EN_N | GPIO3 (JMC1 pin 6); RDR2 10 kΩ pull-up to V3V3 |
| 2 | MS1 | GND | UART address bit 0 = 0 |
| 3 | MS2 | GND | UART address bit 1 = 0 (address 0) |
| 4 | PDN_UART | PDN_UART | UART net: RDR1 from GPIO2, direct to GPIO17 |
| 5 | PDN_UART | PDN_UART | Same net as pin 4 (duplicate pad on some modules; tie both) |
| 6 | CLK | GND | Tie to GND to use the internal oscillator |
| 7 | STEP | STEP | GPIO0 (JMC1 pin 3) |
| 8 | DIR | DIR | GPIO1 (JMC1 pin 4) |

**JDR2 (power / motor row)**

| Pin | Module pad | Net | Connect to |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | VM | V24 | 24 V; CDR1 100 µF/50 V bulk cap nearby |
| 2 | GND | GND | Ground (power return) |
| 3 | 2B | M_2B | Motor connector JDR3 pin 4 |
| 4 | 2A | M_2A | Motor connector JDR3 pin 3 |
| 5 | 1A | M_1A | Motor connector JDR3 pin 1 |
| 6 | 1B | M_1B | Motor connector JDR3 pin 2 |
| 7 | VIO | V3V3 | Logic supply 3.3 V |
| 8 | GND | GND | Ground |

- CDR1 (100 µF/50 V) as the local VM bulk capacitor.
- **Motor connector:** JDR3, JST-XH 4-pin, right-angle / side entry (S4B-XH-A, LCSC C157925, 3 A rated). The lead leaves sideways, so put JDR3 at a board edge facing the motor and keep the opening unobstructed. It mates with the same XHP-4 housing as the vertical B4B-XH-A it replaces. Salvaged printer motors are commonly XH-terminated, but check yours. Confirm coil pairing (1A/1B and 2A/2B) with a meter before connecting.
- StallGuard is used for homing only, via UART; no DIAG GPIO is routed.

## 8. Servo Outputs (Elbow only)

JSV1–JSV3: 3-pin male headers (signal, V6, GND), 1 kΩ series resistor on each signal line, V6 from the buck. Not populated on other nodes. V6 is 6.42 V, inside the 4.8–7.2 V range of MG996R-class servos.

## 9. Interboard Connectors

| Block | Pitch | Pos. | Carries | LCSC | Listed rating |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| JPW1 (in), JPW2 (out) | 5.08 mm | 2 | 24 V, GND | C474952 | 24 A, 12–22 AWG |
| JCN1 (in), JCN2 (out) | 3.5 mm | 2 | CAN_H, CAN_L | C474892 | 10 A, 18–24 AWG |

- Ratings are the LCSC listing's figures for these generic parts, not independently verified. A 3.5 mm block at 6–8 A would be at its listed limit, which is why power uses 5.08 mm.
- **Two different pitches/colours on purpose:** the XT30 keying that stopped 24 V reaching the CAN pins is gone, and 24 V on a CAN transceiver pin destroys it. Different pitches make the mistake harder.
- One block pair per function so every terminal takes one wire; do not stack two wires in a terminal.
- Wire: 16–18 AWG silicone flex for 24 V/GND (16 AWG on the Base input segment), 22–24 AWG twisted pair for CAN. Use ferrules; add a cable-tie anchor next to each block because Shoulder/Elbow joints flex.
- CAN reference ground is the 24 V return, so the power and CAN runs follow the same chain.

## 10. Component List (BOM)

Reference designators: the buck stage (UBK1) uses the XL4016 datasheet's own names (L1, D1, CIN, C1, CC, C2, COUT, CFF, R1, R2); the 5 V LDO parts are CIN_LD and COUT_LD (LM2940 datasheet CIN/COUT, suffixed to stay unique).

SixEyes – Technical Reference 3

---

1. Project Purpose & Scope

---

SixEyes is a low-cost, open-source, research robotic arm platform
designed for Vision–Language–Action (VLA) experimentation.

The system focuses on:

• Collecting high-quality multimodal datasets
• Enabling real-time VLA policy execution
• Using accessible, reusable hardware
• Maintaining deterministic low-level control

The system architecture intentionally separates:

Embedded control (real-time)
from
AI inference (high compute).

Embedded hardware focuses strictly on sensing,
motor control, and safety.

All perception, planning, and machine learning
runs on an external laptop.

Two hardware generations exist:

Alpha
  Through-hole DevKit carrier.
  Single-layer protoboard.
  External TMC2209 modules.

Beta Rev2 (this document)
  60×60 mm 4-layer custom PCB.
  Integrated MCU, drivers, buck converters.
  ESP32-S3-WROOM-1-N16R8 (16 MB Flash, 8 MB Octal PSRAM).

This document reflects Beta Rev2.
For Alpha hardware details see docs/hardware/alpha/.

2. High-Level System Architecture

---

2.1 Distributed Control Philosophy

Layer: Low-Level Control
Device: Node0 — ESP32-S3-WROOM-1-N16R8 (Beta PCB)
Responsibilities:
• 400 Hz stepper timing (4 axes)
• Servo PWM control (3 channels)
• StallGuard2 sensorless homing
• Safety supervision and heartbeat

Layer: Communication Layer
Device: USB-CDC serial link (Native OTG via J_USB1)
Responsibilities:
• Deterministic command transfer
• Telemetry streaming to ROS2

Layer: High-Level Intelligence
Device: Laptop
Responsibilities:
• Vision processing
• VLA policy inference
• Dataset logging
• Motion planning

Layer: Orchestration
Device: ROS2 (Laptop)
Responsibilities:
• Node communication
• Dataset synchronization
• Experiment tooling

---

2.2 NodeMesh Topology

In NodeMesh mode the system spans four MCUs:

Node0 — Beta PCB (ESP32-S3-WROOM-1-N16R8)
  Role: Real-time orchestrator.
  Runs 400 Hz control loop.
  Aggregates joint states and vision features.
  Logs experience packets to MicroSD.

Node1 — ESP32-C6 SuperMini (offboard, wired to Node0)
  Role: Leader/teleoperation input board.
  Reads 6× potentiometers at 100 Hz ADC.
  Sends absolute joint targets at 921,600 baud over UART.

Node2 — ESP32-CAM (global perspective camera, wireless)
  Role: Global camera node.
  Sends 128-byte brightness histogram feature vectors
  via ESP-NOW channel 6 to Node0.

Node3 — ESP32-CAM (wrist/eye-in-hand camera, wireless)
  Role: Wrist camera node.
  Same protocol as Node2.

Node0 discards camera frames older than 300 ms
before merging into the IK/experience packet.

3. Hardware Architecture

---

3.1 Leader Arm (Teleoperation Controller)

The leader arm captures human motion input using potentiometers.
These analog signals represent target joint positions.

MCU:
ESP32-C6 SuperMini (Node1)

ADC Domain:
ADC1 only is used to avoid WiFi interference issues.

Inputs:
6× potentiometers.

Leader Pinout (ESP32-C6 Node1):

Pot 1  → GPIO0
Pot 2  → GPIO1
Pot 3  → GPIO2
Pot 4  → GPIO3
Pot 5  → GPIO4
Pot 6  → GPIO6

UART Link to Node0 (Follower):

Node1 TX → (J_LDR Pin 2) → Node0 GPIO42
Node1 RX → (J_LDR Pin 1) → Node0 GPIO41

Baud:
921,600 baud, 8N1.
Magic-number scan + CRC-CCITT (poly 0x1021) framing.
Packets carry absolute joint targets for all 6 joints.

Power:
Node1 is powered via J_LDR_PWR on the Beta PCB.
5V / GND from the Beta 5V logic rail.
10 µF ceramic bypass cap at the header.

---

3.2 Follower Arm (Actuated Robot Arm)

The follower arm executes motion using stepper motors
and servo actuators.

Degrees of Freedom:

Base            → 1× NEMA17 (J1)
Shoulder A      → 1× NEMA17 (J2, torque split)
Shoulder B      → 1× NEMA17 (J3, torque split)
Elbow           → 1× NEMA17 (J4)
Wrist Pitch     → 1× Servo (J_SV1)
Wrist Yaw       → 1× Servo (J_SV2)
Gripper         → 1× Servo (J_SV3)

Total:

• 4 stepper channels
• 3 servo channels
• 6 DOF system

IK Parameters:

Upper arm link = 140 mm
Forearm link   = 140 mm
2-link planar IK computed on Node0.

---

3.3 Stepper Motor Drivers (Beta Rev2)

Drivers:

4× TMC2209-LA (QFN-28, direct IC on Beta PCB, 2×2 grid)

Configuration:

• PDN_UART mode enabled (single-wire UART per driver)
• StallGuard2 enabled (DIAG pins routed)
• CoolStep adaptive current scaling available
• SpreadCycle / StealthChop2 configurable per driver
• VIO = 3.3V
• VM  = 24V

Global enable:
GPIO4 → DRV_EN_ALL (active LOW, shared to all 4 drivers EN pins).

PDN_UART wiring (per driver):
1 kΩ series resistor + 4.7 kΩ pull-up to 3.3V.

DIAG wiring (per driver):
47 kΩ pull-up to 3.3V (open-drain output — pull-up is mandatory).

Beta Follower GPIO — TMC2209 Pin Assignment:

J1 – Base

STEP     → GPIO5
DIR      → GPIO6
PDN_UART → GPIO7   (1 kΩ series + 4.7 kΩ pull-up)
DIAG     ← GPIO1   (47 kΩ pull-up, StallGuard2 / fault)

J2 – Shoulder A

STEP     → GPIO8
DIR      → GPIO9
PDN_UART → GPIO14  (1 kΩ series + 4.7 kΩ pull-up)
DIAG     ← GPIO2   (47 kΩ pull-up, StallGuard2 / fault)

J3 – Shoulder B

STEP     → GPIO15
DIR      → GPIO16
PDN_UART → GPIO17  (1 kΩ series + 4.7 kΩ pull-up)
DIAG     ← GPIO3   (47 kΩ pull-up, StallGuard2 / fault)

J4 – Elbow

STEP     → GPIO18
DIR      → GPIO21
PDN_UART → GPIO47  (1 kΩ series + 4.7 kΩ pull-up)
DIAG     ← GPIO48  (47 kΩ pull-up, StallGuard2 / fault)

Global Enable:

EN_ALL   → GPIO4   (active LOW)

3.4 Servo Outputs

Servos operate on the dedicated 6.6V rail.

Servo GPIO (Beta Rev2):

Wrist Pitch → GPIO38  (J_SV1)
Wrist Yaw   → GPIO39  (J_SV2)
Gripper     → GPIO40  (J_SV3)

PWM generation via ESP32-S3 LEDC / MCPWM peripheral.

Servo Power:

• Powered from 6.6V rail (TPS54540B buck on PCB)
• Connector type: 2.54 mm 3-pin header per channel
• Pinout per header: Signal / 6.6V / GND

Servo Current Budget:

Continuous: 5.0 A total (3 servos)
Stall peak:  7.0 A

---

3.5 Leader–Follower Communication

Communication between Node1 (leader) and Node0 (follower)
uses a dedicated hardware UART.

Node0 (Follower) Side:

UART RX (Node1 TX) → GPIO42
UART TX (Node1 RX) → GPIO41

Baud rate: 921,600, 8N1.

Packet framing: magic-number scan + CRC-CCITT (poly 0x1021).
Non-blocking async receive on Node0.

Connector: J_LDR — 2.54 mm 4-pin header (South Edge of PCB).
Pinout: TX, RX, GND, spare.

Benefits:

• Deterministic latency
• Works without laptop
• Allows teleoperation fallback mode

---

3.6 Camera Architecture (NodeMesh)

In NodeMesh mode, cameras are Node2 and Node3 (ESP32-CAM modules).
They communicate wirelessly to Node0.

Camera nodes are NOT wired to the Beta PCB data lines.
Power only is supplied via J_CAM1 and J_CAM2 (5V / GND).

Architecture:

Node2 (Global Cam)  → ESP-NOW channel 6 → Node0
Node3 (Wrist Cam)   → ESP-NOW channel 6 → Node0

Data payload:
128-byte brightness histogram feature vector embedded in
ExperiencePacket (magic 0x4E4D5650).

Freshness gate:
Node0 discards frames older than 300 ms before IK merge.

Power connectors on Beta PCB:

J_CAM1 — JST XH 2.5mm 2-pin — Node2 5V / GND (South Edge)
J_CAM2 — JST XH 2.5mm 2-pin — Node3 5V / GND (South Edge)

10 µF ceramic bypass cap at each camera header
to absorb ESP-NOW TX burst (~200 mA step).

In standalone SixEyes mode (without NodeMesh),
cameras may be connected directly to the laptop via USB.

3.7 MicroSD Logging

Hardware SPI. FAT32, 32 kB clusters.
1 MB pre-allocated circular buffer for experience packets.
~300 B per experience packet.
SD flush every 8 writes to reduce blocking.

SD GPIO (Beta Rev2):

CS   → GPIO10  (10 kΩ pull-up)
MOSI → GPIO11  (10 kΩ pull-up)
SCK  → GPIO12
MISO → GPIO13  (10 kΩ pull-up)

Connector: Hirose DM3D-SF MicroSD push-pull (interior zone).

---

3.8 Dual USB-C Interface

Port 1 — J_USB1 (West Edge)
  Native USB-OTG.
  GPIO19 (D-) / GPIO20 (D+).
  90 Ω differential pair, routed on L1 only.
  Used for: ROS2 USB-CDC bridge, telemetry streaming,
  VLA dataset logging.

Port 2 — J_USB2 (West Edge)
  CH340K UART bridge.
  GPIO43 (UART0 TXD) / GPIO44 (UART0 RXD).
  DTR/RTS auto-reset for PlatformIO firmware upload.
  Eliminates need for external USB-serial programmer.

4. Power Architecture

---

4.1 Main Power Input

Primary input:

24V DC via XT30 right-angle connector (J_PWR, West Edge).
Recommended supply: ≥10A.

This rail directly powers:

• 4× TMC2209 VM pins (direct, fused)
• 6.6V buck converter input
• 5.0V buck converter input

---

4.2 6.6V Rail (Servo Power)

Regulator:

TPS54540B — 5.5A synchronous buck, HSOP-8-EP.
Integrated on Beta PCB.

Output Voltage:

6.6V
(R_top = 102 kΩ, R_bot = 14 kΩ, V_ref = 0.8V)

Purpose:

• Power 3× MG996R / MG995 class servos

Inductor:
Shielded iron-powder / metal composite.
L = 4.7 µH, I_sat > 9.0 A.
Footprint ≥ 10×10 mm (e.g. Bourns SRR1260, Sumida CDEP134).

Output Capacitance:
≥ 1000 µF low-ESR SMD electrolytic
(2× 470 µF / 16V in parallel acceptable)
+ 10 µF ceramic bank at servo headers.

---

4.3 5.0V Rail (Logic & Peripheral Domain)

Regulator:

LMR14030S — 3A asynchronous buck, HSOP-8-EP.
Integrated on Beta PCB.

Loads and worst-case current budget:

Node2 ESP32-CAM (global camera, ESP-NOW TX)    200 mA typ / 400 mA peak
Node3 ESP32-CAM (wrist camera, ESP-NOW TX)     200 mA typ / 400 mA peak
Node1 ESP32-C6 offboard (leader UART + RF)     100 mA typ / 250 mA peak
MIC5219-3.3 LDO input (ESP32-S3 + TMC VIO)    400 mA typ / 620 mA peak

Total 5.0V rail:                               900 mA typ / 1670 mA peak

LMR14030S 3A rating provides >1.3× headroom over worst-case peak.
Inductor: 4.7 µH, I_sat > 3.5A.
Output: ≥ 100 µF SMD electrolytic + ceramic bank.

---

4.4 3.3V Rail (Logic Domain)

Regulator:

MIC5219-3.3YM5 — 1A LDO, SOT-23-5.
Powered from 5.0V rail.

Loads:

ESP32-S3-WROOM-1 core + RF    500 mA peak
4× TMC2209 VIO (15 mA each)   60 mA peak
Pull-up resistor networks      20 mA peak

Total:                         580 mA peak

MIC5219-3.3 1A rating provides ~420 mA headroom.

Thermal note:
Worst-case dissipation P_D = (5.0 - 3.3) V × 0.58 A ≈ 1.0 W.
Ensure adequate copper spreading under SOT-23-5 pad.

Input cap:  ≥ 10 µF ceramic (0805) on +5V input pin.
Output cap: 22 µF ceramic (0805) + 100 nF X7R (0603).

---

4.5 Power Rail Summary

24V Rail
  → 4× TMC2209 VM (direct, fused)
  → TPS54540B 6.6V buck input
  → LMR14030S 5.0V buck input

6.6V Rail (TPS54540B)
  → 3× Servo channels (J_SV1–J_SV3)

5.0V Rail (LMR14030S)
  → Node1 leader board (J_LDR_PWR)
  → Node2 camera (J_CAM1)
  → Node3 camera (J_CAM2)
  → MIC5219-3.3 LDO input

3.3V Rail (MIC5219-3.3)
  → ESP32-S3-WROOM-1 core logic
  → 4× TMC2209 VIO
  → Pull-up networks

---

4.6 24V Motor Rail Filtering (per TMC2209)

Placed within 2 mm of IC package edge:

Bulk:
100 µF / 35V low-ESR SMD electrolytic
(Panasonic FR or Nichicon UWX, case D / 7.3 mm diameter).
DO NOT substitute tantalum — motor back-EMF causes catastrophic failure.

HF bypass:
100 nF X7R 50V (0603), trace length to VM pin ≤ 1 mm.

---

4.7 Grounding Strategy

4-layer PCB stackup:

L1 — Signal / Component (1 oz)
L2 — Solid unbroken GND plane (1 oz) — primary return path
L3 — Split power plane (24V, 6.6V, 5V, 3.3V copper polygons)
L4 — Signal escape / thermal spreading (1 oz)

Rules:

• Servo return currents must not share trace segments with MCU signal GND.
  Both terminate at L2 via separate vias.
• Buck switching node polygons on L1 must not be crossed by signal traces.
• GND stitching vias L1→L2 every ~5 mm at board perimeter.
• GND stitching vias along full length of USB differential pair routes.
• GND stitching vias adjacent to all buck switching polygons.

9. Communication Protocols

---

9.1 Safety Heartbeat (ASCII, hardware-agnostic)

ROS2 → ESP32:
  HB:<source_id>,<seq>\n
  Rate: ≥ 50 Hz

ESP32 → ROS2:
  SB:<fault_bitmask>,<motors_en>,<ros2_alive>\n
  Rate: 10 Hz

Timeout: >500 ms → motors disabled, latched until RESET_FAULT.

---

9.2 JSON Command Protocol (VLA Inference Mode)

Line-delimited, \n framed, max 512 bytes per message.

Commands (ROS2 → ESP32):
  MOTOR_TARGET      — stepper position targets
  SERVO_TARGET      — servo angle targets
  ENABLE_MOTORS     — arm motors
  RESET_FAULT       — clear latched fault
  HOME_ZERO         — zero all axes
  HOME_STALLGUARD   — sensorless homing via StallGuard2
  TUNE_PID          — adjust control loop gains

---

9.3 Teleoperation UART Protocol

Joint State (Node1 → Node0, 921,600 baud):
  JOINT_STATE      — leader joint angles → follower mirrors

Telemetry (Node0 → host, USB-CDC):
  TELEMETRY_STATE  — current joint positions + status

Packet framing:
  Magic-number scan + CRC-CCITT (poly 0x1021).

11. Safety Architecture

---

Safety principles:

• Motors disabled by default at boot.
• Motion only enabled after heartbeat is established.
• RESET_FAULT required to re-enable after any fault.
• Heartbeat must be active before ENABLE_MOTORS accepted.

Heartbeat timeout: 500 ms.

Safety control:
DRV_EN_ALL (GPIO4) controlled by safety supervisor task.
Active LOW — pulled LOW by ESP32 to disable all drivers simultaneously.

If laptop communication stops:
• All drivers disabled via DRV_EN_ALL.
• Motion halts immediately.
• State latched until RESET_FAULT received.

DIAG pin monitoring:
GPIO1 / 2 / 3 / 48 are TMC2209 StallGuard2 / fault flags.
Open-drain outputs with 47 kΩ pull-ups on PCB.
Firmware reads these to detect stalls and log fault events.

Beta PCB safety rules (never violate):

• Forbidden GPIOs (26/33/34/35/36/37) must never appear in firmware.
• DIAG pull-ups (47 kΩ) are mandatory — open-drain without pull-up
  will read indeterminate logic level.
• No tantalum capacitors on the 24V motor rail.

12. Mechanical Design

---

12.1 Performance Targets

Payload:      500 g at full reach
Reach:        500 mm (base rotation axis → TCP, arm horizontal)
Gear ratio:   25:1 cycloidal (all stepper joints)
Structure:    Fully 3D-printed, no ball bearings

---

12.2 Link Length Ratios (AgileX Piper Reference)

Design reference: AgileX Piper 6-DOF arm
  Piper upper arm (a3):       285 mm
  Piper forearm (d4):         251 mm
  Piper wrist-to-TCP (d6):     91 mm
  Piper total reach:          627 mm
  Piper payload:             1500 g

Scale factor for SixEyes (500 mm / 627 mm = 0.798):

  Segment           Piper    SixEyes   Fraction of reach
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Upper arm         285 mm   225 mm    45.0%
  Forearm           251 mm   200 mm    40.0%
  Wrist assembly     91 mm    75 mm    15.0%
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Total             627 mm   500 mm   100.0%

Link ratios:
  Upper arm : Forearm : Wrist = 225 : 200 : 75 ≈ 3.00 : 2.67 : 1.00
  Upper arm : Forearm         = 1.125 : 1

The slightly longer upper arm (vs equal-length links) follows Piper's
proportions and improves dexterity in the mid-workspace without
increasing elbow torque demand at full extension.

IK constants (Node0 firmware):
  L1 (upper arm) = 225 mm
  L2 (forearm)   = 200 mm
  2-link planar IK on Node0.

NOTE: The previous Technical Reference (v2) used 140 mm / 140 mm as
placeholder IK constants. These are superseded by the values above.

---

12.3 Wrist Assembly Breakdown (75 mm total)

Kinematic chain from elbow outward:

  Elbow flange → Wrist Yaw pivot:    23 mm
  Wrist Yaw → Wrist Pitch pivot:     27 mm
  Wrist Pitch pivot → TCP:           25 mm
  ─────────────────────────────────────────
  Total wrist assembly:              75 mm

Joint order:
  J1  Base yaw          Stepper + cycloidal 25:1
  J2  Shoulder pitch    Stepper + cycloidal 25:1  (torque split with J3)
  J3  Shoulder pitch B  Stepper + cycloidal 25:1  (torque split partner)
  J4  Elbow pitch       Stepper + cycloidal 25:1
  J5  Wrist Yaw         MG996R servo  ← FURTHER from gripper
  J6  Wrist Pitch       MG996R servo  ← CLOSER to gripper
  J7  Gripper           MG996R servo

---

12.4 Wrist Joint Ordering Rationale

Wrist Pitch (J6) is placed closer to the gripper than Wrist Yaw (J5).

Reason: maximise lifting capacity within MG996R torque limits.

MG996R specifications:
  Stall torque:      13 kg·cm = 1.27 N·m  (at 6.6V)
  Operating torque:  ~0.90 N·m (continuous safe)

With Pitch closer to gripper (this design):
  Load on pitch = gripper assembly (~100 g) + payload (500 g) = 600 g
  Moment arm    = 25 mm (pitch pivot → TCP)
  Required τ    = 0.6 kg × 9.81 m/s² × 0.025 m = 0.147 N·m
  Stall margin  = 1.27 / 0.147 = 8.6×
  Operating margin = 0.90 / 0.147 = 6.1×

If ordering were reversed (Pitch further from gripper, ~50 mm from TCP):
  Load on pitch = yaw assembly (~80 g) + gripper (100 g) + payload (500 g) = 680 g
  Moment arm    = 50 mm
  Required τ    = 0.68 × 9.81 × 0.050 = 0.334 N·m
  Stall margin  = 1.27 / 0.334 = 3.8×
  Operating margin = 0.90 / 0.334 = 2.7×  ← dangerously low at high duty cycle

The current ordering (Yaw → Pitch → Gripper) reduces the pitch torque
demand by 2.3× relative to the reversed ordering, providing safe margin
for the MG996R under 500 g payload at maximum reach.

Wrist Yaw (J5) rotates approximately about the forearm's long axis in
mid-pose; the gravitational loading on yaw is pose-dependent and lower
than pitch when the arm is extended horizontally for lifting tasks.

---

12.5 Per-Joint Torque Budget (500 g at 500 mm, arm horizontal)

Estimates assume 3D-printed link masses: upper arm ~200 g, forearm ~150 g,
wrist assembly ~80 g, gripper ~100 g.

Joint   Type        Worst-case τ    Available τ     Margin
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
J1 Base  Cycloidal  2.7 N·m *       10.0 N·m (est)  3.7×
J2+J3   Dual cyclo  3.2 N·m         20.0 N·m (est)  6.3×
J4 Elbow Cycloidal  0.7 N·m         10.0 N·m (est) 14.3×
J5 Yaw   MG996R     0.22 N·m (est)   1.27 N·m       5.8×
J6 Pitch MG996R     0.147 N·m        1.27 N·m       8.6×
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

* J1 base torque is inertial/dynamic (arm rotating); gravitational
  load on base is zero when arm is symmetric. Figure shown is for
  worst-case deceleration at 400 Hz control rate.

Available cycloidal torque estimates assume NEMA17 holding torque
~0.4 N·m × 25:1 ratio. Actual value depends on motor current setting
(TMC2209 RMS current config) and temperature derating.

---

12.6 Shoulder Joint

Two NEMA17 motors are used (J2 and J3).

Reasons:
• Shoulder (J2/J3) bears the highest sustained gravitational torque
  in the kinematic chain (full arm + payload at max reach).
• Torque split across two cycloidal drives improves reliability
  and halves shaft stress on each motor.

Gear reduction: 25:1 cycloidal per motor (effectively 2 × 10 N·m at joint).

13. Mechanical Design Constraints

---

• Cycloidal gearboxes are fully 3D-printed.
• Software compensates for backlash.
• StallGuard2 used for sensorless homing (no limit switches required).
• Wiring routed internally where possible.
• Camera mounting (Node3) must avoid wrist occlusion.
• Beta PCB antenna (North Edge): 2 mm overhang past board edge.
  15×10 mm copper keep-out zone under antenna, all 4 layers.

14. Cost and Sustainability Goals

---

Reuse hardware wherever possible.

• NEMA17 motors from old 3D printers

Structural components:

• Entirely 3D-printed

~$250 USD

• ROS2 AI control stack on laptop
• StallGuard2 sensorless homing (DIAG on GPIO1/2/3/48)

16. Summary Statement

---

SixEyes is a reproducible,
low-cost research platform for
Vision–Language–Action robotics.

The embedded system focuses on:

deterministic control,
hardware safety,
and real-time actuation.

All intelligence is handled externally
by a laptop running ROS2 and VLA models.

This separation keeps the hardware simple,
robust, and accessible to researchers.
